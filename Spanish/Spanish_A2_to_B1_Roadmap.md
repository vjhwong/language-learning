# Spanish A2 → B1 Roadmap

**Starting point:** approximately A2, based on your estimate and the practice material in this folder.  
**Goal:** practical, independent B1 Spanish—not perfect grammar or native-like fluency.

## What “B1” means

At B1, you can understand the main points of clear speech and writing on familiar subjects, handle most predictable situations while travelling, and produce connected, understandable speech and writing about experiences, plans, opinions, and familiar topics. You can explain a problem and give reasons, even if you still make errors or need to rephrase.

The Instituto Cervantes’ DELE B1 framework checks reading, listening, writing, and speaking. Its B1 description includes coherent texts about experiences, events, wishes, plans, and opinions. So our progress checks will include all four skills, not grammar exercises alone.

## Roadmap

Treat these as learning units, not fixed weeks. We can spend more time where you need it and revisit earlier topics in later stories.

### 1. Make A2 reliable

- Talk about routines, people, places, likes, needs, and simple plans.
- Use present tense, *ser/estar/hay*, common irregular verbs, articles, adjective agreement, and basic prepositions with fewer recurring errors.
- Ask and answer follow-up questions; describe a place or person in connected sentences.
- **Story practice:** everyday scenes with a clear setting, familiar vocabulary, and a small amount of past or future language.
- **Checkpoint:** write 8–10 connected sentences about your life and hold a short conversation about them.

### 2. Tell past events clearly

- Choose between *pretérito indefinido* for completed events and *imperfecto* for background, habits, and descriptions.
- Use common irregular past forms, reflexive verbs, time expressions, and sequencing words such as *primero, luego, mientras, al final*.
- Describe what happened, what things were like, and how you felt.
- **Story practice:** short personal anecdotes, trips, surprises, and childhood memories; identify the event sequence and background details.
- **Checkpoint:** tell a 1–2 minute story and write a short narrative another person can follow.

### 3. Manage everyday situations

- Make requests, arrangements, suggestions, invitations, and comparisons.
- Handle travel, shopping, restaurants, housing, health, appointments, and simple service problems.
- Use *ir a + infinitive*, present tense for near-future plans, *tener que / hay que / poder / querer*, and polite request forms.
- **Story practice:** practical scenarios with a small problem to solve; role-play the conversation after reading.
- **Checkpoint:** complete a familiar real-life role-play and explain what you need or what went wrong.

### 4. Connect ideas and explain opinions

- Link sentences with *porque, por eso, pero, aunque, además, entonces, cuando, si*.
- State an opinion, give one or two reasons, agree or disagree politely, and describe advantages or disadvantages.
- Use common object pronouns and commands in familiar contexts; build more complex sentences with *que* and *cuando*.
- **Story practice:** characters make choices; explain what you think they should do and why.
- **Checkpoint:** give a short opinion with reasons and respond to a follow-up question.

### 5. Talk about possibilities and plans

- Use the simple future and conditional patterns for predictions, intentions, and polite requests.
- Use the present subjunctive in frequent set patterns for wishes, recommendations, and necessity (for example, *quiero que…, es importante que…*).
- Talk about hopes, goals, preferences, and possible outcomes without needing advanced or rare constructions.
- **Story practice:** plans change, characters make predictions, and you choose an ending.
- **Checkpoint:** describe a goal, explain your plan, and discuss what might happen.

### 6. Build B1 independence

- Follow the main point of clear, standard speech on familiar topics and understand short everyday texts, messages, announcements, and narratives.
- Speak at length in a simple, organized way; ask for clarification, repeat or rephrase when needed, and keep a conversation moving.
- Write connected messages and short narratives/opinion texts with a clear beginning, middle, and end.
- **Story practice:** gradually reduce glossaries and scaffolding; include short listening/retelling tasks and open-ended responses.
- **Checkpoint:** complete a mixed four-skill review using unfamiliar but familiar-topic material.

## Our story + worksheet format

Each practice pack can follow this pattern:

1. **Short story:** one clear B1-relevant situation, with useful new phrases repeated in context.
2. **Before reading:** a few prediction questions and a small vocabulary preview.
3. **Comprehension:** main idea, details, sequence, and inference questions.
4. **Language focus:** one main grammar feature plus useful connectors and phrases, drawn from the story.
5. **Practice:** controlled exercises followed by sentence-building or a short translation task.
6. **Personal response:** write or speak about your own experience, view, or alternative ending.
7. **Answer key and feedback:** answers, brief explanations, and corrections that focus on recurring patterns.
8. **Optional listening/speaking:** read-aloud/audio-friendly version, retelling prompts, and a role-play or follow-up conversation.

Stories are a good central method for you: they put grammar and vocabulary into meaningful situations, and the worksheet can move from understanding to using the language yourself. To reach B1 well, we should pair reading stories with regular listening, speaking, and writing, because stories alone cannot demonstrate those skills.

## B1 “graduation” checklist

You are ready to call yourself approximately B1 when you can usually:

- Understand the main points and relevant details in clear, standard material about familiar subjects.
- Take part in common travel and everyday situations without scripting every sentence.
- Tell a connected story in past tenses, including background and events.
- Describe plans, experiences, preferences, and goals; give reasons for opinions.
- Write a clear, connected message or short text about a familiar topic.
- Keep a conversation going with questions, clarification, and rephrasing when you lack a word.
- Use a useful range of present, past, and future language, with errors that do not usually block understanding.

This is a practical progress benchmark, not an official certificate. An official DELE B1 certificate requires the Instituto Cervantes exam.

## How we’ll track progress

After each story pack, I can note: **what you understood, what you could produce, recurring errors, and the next useful target**. Every few packs, we can revisit an earlier task to see whether you can do it more independently. We’ll begin with a short A2 review story and adjust the difficulty based on your answers.
